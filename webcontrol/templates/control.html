AI control (Claude)

rails — limits the AI can never change Rails cap what the AI may command; the PV interlock still caps temperature for everyone, the AI included. An empty number means “not set”: for a min / max pair the AI then may not change that quantity at all; for a step, rate or arm time it means no limit. Live values are shown next to each item. Save sends the whole set; the service refuses it if the rails were saved somewhere else since this form was filled in.
recent AI activity
Lets a Claude session work the heaters, the LN₂ fill valves and the gas valves through the AI control service (port 8089) — only in a group armed here, and only within the rails. Arming or disarming moves nothing: disarming only stops further AI changes, and every setpoint and valve stays where it is. Arms come up off every time the AI service starts and lapse after the rails' arm time; the service also disarms by itself (it never re-arms) on a slow-control restart, stale data or a lost broker connection, and the heaters on a PV interlock trip or an interlock alert. Rails cap what the AI may command; the PV interlock still caps temperature for everyone. The AI's tools can read the rails but cannot change them or arm anything, and cannot reach the turbos, the interlock limits, the valve and turbo gates or the Sequencer. Gas valves need the PLC valve gate in the Pneumatic card enabled as well — both must be on. An LN₂ valve the AI opened is closed by the service when the rail's max open time runs out, armed or not. No per-click confirmation: arming is the safety step.

If any LabJack RTD reads outside [min, max] or its data is stale, the heater PIDs are driven to 0 % output. The default [77 K, 310 K] covers LN₂ → just above room temp and catches sensor faults (e.g. broken RTD reading -100000 K). To bake out, raise the max to your target. Solenoid valves — LN₂ manifold AI armed

“actual” is the energised state read back from the PLC; “desired” is the last command sent. Auto-open / auto-close arm the PLC ladder’s autofill enables for that vessel.

Pneumatic valves — gas handling AI armed

Open / close only work while valve control is enabled — the buttons at the top of the card. The lock is in the PLC driver, so it holds for the Sequencer and anything else on the bus, not only this page; control comes up disabled every time the slow-control service starts, and disabling it moves no valve. Once control is on there is no per-click confirmation. Open / close always mean the valve, not the relay. Ballast and bottle are normally open: their relay is energised to close them, so with the PLC outputs off they sit open. The pump valve is normally closed. “actual” is worked out from the PLC output (Y105–Y107) read back, “desired” from the register (DS151–DS153), and “relay” is the output itself. The ladder has no auto-open, auto-close or timer behind these, so a valve stays where it was last put — through a service restart too.

Heaters — PID loops AI armed

Everything is in Kelvin. Expression fields accept arithmetic over the aliases rtd1..rtd6, tc1..tc4 (each = the latest value_k in K) plus direct register refs DF<n> / DS<n> / C<n> — e.g. rtd1 + 5 (5 K above the LJ nozzle RTD). The expression is re-evaluated each poll; the K result is converted to °C only when written to the CLICK's DF (which stores temperatures in °C). Caveat: DF<n> returns the raw register contents — for the PID's temperature DFs that's °C, so add 273.15 if mixing with a Kelvin alias.

Heater supply — R&S NGE103 AI armed

…
hold
The DC supply behind the heaters — NGE channel n feeds heater n; the PLC still PWMs each one. Hold keeps every channel at what is set here: a channel that drops out (protection trip, supply power-cycled, switched at the front panel) is switched back on, and one switched on at the front panel is switched off again. Turn hold off to work at the front panel. Stopping the slow-control service leaves the supply as it is. 33.6 W per channel: the supply caps the current limit at 33.6 W ÷ V — 1.40 A at 24 V, whatever is asked — so a 36 W heater runs current-limited (CC) at about 22.4 V. The service keeps over-power protection and the electronic fuse off, because those switch a channel off where plain current limiting is wanted. V / A / W are instantaneous and catch the heater mid-PWM-cycle; the Register page has their 60 s averages.

Turbo pumps — Leybold TURBOVAC

On / off / release only work while turbo control is enabled — the buttons at the top of the card. The lock is in the turbo driver, so it holds for the Sequencer and anything else on the bus too; it comes up disabled every time the slow-control service starts, and disabling it starts, stops or releases nothing. Once it is on there is no per-click confirmation. on / off start and stop that pump over its USB link and hold it there: the slow control repeats the command with every telegram, about every 2 s. release gives it back, about 10 s later, to whatever else commands it — its TURBOLAB, or its own X1 start input (nothing plugged in there means stop). A pump on its TURBOLAB Core's RS 485 lead ignores all of this: the Core outranks USB, and the card says the pump does not report the bus in control. Both turbos' leads are out (outer since 2026-10-12, GHS since 2026-10-14), so they are the slow control's. “keeps its command without the Pi” (keep_command in config.yaml): while it is held, that pump's watchdog (parameter 182) is 0, so it stays running — or stopped — with the Pi, the service or the USB cable gone; released, it gets its 10 s back. Otherwise holding is not locking: a pump that hears nothing for 10 s lets go by itself. After a service restart a hold carries on if the pump was out of touch for less than two minutes, or for any time if it kept the command itself; after longer it is left alone and the card says so. Start, stop and that one watchdog setting are all that can be sent — never a speed or anything else — and only while turbovac.allow_control is true. A pump is recognised by its own serial number (turbovac.pumps), not by the USB port it is on, so swapping cables cannot swap the names or the buttons. A pump whose supply is cut stays on USB while its rotor runs down (it powers its own electronics), then shows as not connected. NORMAL means at normal-operation speed (90 % of the setpoint and above); RUNNING means the drive is on but the rotor is held below that. The temperatures are the pump's own (motor, bearing, converter); “last entry” is the newest line of the pump's error memory, with how many running-hours ago it was made; “last event” is the latest change the service saw — a pump found or lost, a command, a change of state, a new error entry.
